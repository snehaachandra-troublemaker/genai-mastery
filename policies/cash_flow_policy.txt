CASH FLOW MANAGEMENT POLICY

1. CASH COLLECTION TARGETS
Monthly collection target: 95% of current receivables
  Striving to collect nearly all invoices that fall due within the 
  calendar month to maintain high liquidity.

Key metrics:
- DSO (Days Sales Outstanding): Target <35 days
  The average number of days it takes for the company to collect 
  payment after a sale has been made.
- Collection effectiveness: >92%
  A measure of the company's ability to collect accounts receivable 
  from its customers during a specific period.
- Bad debt: <1% of revenue
  Minimizing the amount of receivables that are written off as 
  uncollectible to protect the company's bottom line.

2. PAYMENT PRIORITIZATION
Outgoing payments prioritized as follows:
1. Payroll (bi-weekly, non-negotiable)
   Ensuring all employees are paid accurately and on time to 
   maintain morale and legal compliance.
2. Statutory payments (taxes, regulatory fees)
   Meeting all local, state, and federal tax obligations to avoid 
   penalties and interest charges.
3. Critical vendors (sole source, strategic partners)
   Paying essential suppliers whose goods or services are 
   integral to continued business operations.
4. Standard vendors (Net 30 terms)
   Settling balances with general suppliers according to agreed-upon 
   contractual timelines.
5. Discretionary expenses
   Allocating remaining funds to non-essential items such as 
   travel, entertainment, and optional office upgrades.

3. WORKING CAPITAL REQUIREMENTS
Minimum cash balance: $500K operating buffer
  The baseline amount of liquid funds kept on hand to cover 
  unexpected expenses or short-term revenue dips.

Cash forecasting:
- Daily: Cash position review
  Real-time monitoring of bank balances and pending transactions 
  to manage day-to-day liquidity.
- Weekly: 4-week rolling forecast
  Short-term projection of anticipated inflows and outflows to 
  plan for upcoming payroll and vendor cycles.
- Monthly: 12-week detailed forecast
  Medium-term financial planning to align cash resources with 
  quarterly business objectives.
- Quarterly: Annual forecast update
  Long-range analysis to ensure the company's cash strategy 
  supports its yearly growth and investment targets.

4. VENDOR PAYMENT SCHEDULING
Payments processed:
- Tuesday and Thursday each week
  Standardizing disbursement days to streamline accounting 
  workflows and optimize bank communication.
- Cut-off time: 2 PM EST day before processing
  Deadlines for submitting and approving invoices to ensure they 
  are included in the next scheduled payment run.
- ACH payments: 2-day clearing
  Electronic payments that typically take forty-eight hours to 
  be reflected in the vendor's bank account.
- Wire transfers: Same day (fee: $25)
  Immediate funds transfer available for urgent payments, 
  subject to a bank-mandated processing charge.

5. EARLY PAYMENT DISCOUNTS
Eligible for discounts if:
- 2/10 Net 30: 2% discount for payment within 10 days
  The standard incentive structure for capturing savings by 
  accelerating the payment of vendor invoices.
- Minimum invoice amount: $1,000
  Restricting the administration of early payment discounts 
  to higher-value transactions for maximum efficiency.
- Approval required from Treasury
  The central finance team must authorize the early release 
  of funds based on current cash availability.

Savings target: $50K annually through early payment discounts
  An objective to reduce overall procurement costs by 
  proactively managing the payment schedule.

6. LATE PAYMENT FEES
Applied to customer invoices:
- Grace period: 5 days past due date
  Allowing a short window after the deadline before late 
  charges are officially calculated and applied.
- Fee: 1.5% per month (18% APR) on outstanding balance
  The interest rate charged on overdue amounts to compensate 
  the company for the delay in cash receipt.
- Communicated on invoice and statements
  Ensuring transparency by clearly stating the late fee 
  policy on all customer-facing financial documents.

7. CREDIT LINE MANAGEMENT
Revolving credit facility: $2M
  Access to emergency funding to bridge period gaps between 
  expenditures and revenue collection.
- Usage triggers: Cash below $300K threshold
  Automatic notification to tap into the credit line if 
  liquid reserves fall below a safe operating level.
- Interest rate: SOFR + 2.5%
  The cost of borrowing calculated as a spread over the 
  Secured Overnight Financing Rate reference index.
- Review: Quarterly with CFO
  Periodic evaluation of the credit facility's terms and 
  usage to ensure it remains a cost-effective safety net.

8. CASH SURPLUS INVESTMENT
Cash exceeding $1M operating buffer:
- Short-term investments: Treasury bills, money market
  Parking excess capital in highly liquid and low-risk 
  instruments to generate modest interest returns.
- Approval: CFO
  The Chief Financial Officer must sign off on any 
  investment strategies involving corporate cash reserves.
- Liquidity requirement: 30-day max maturity
  Ensuring invested funds can be quickly converted back 
  to cash if the business needs them within a month.

9. CURRENCY RISK MANAGEMENT
For international transactions >$100K:
- Forward contracts to hedge FX risk
  Financial instruments that lock in exchange rates for 
  future dates to protect against currency volatility.
- Approval: Treasury team
  Specialized oversight required to manage complex derivative 
  trades and ensure they meet risk parameters.
- Review: Monthly
  Regular assessment of hedging effectiveness and 
  adjustments based on changing market conditions.

10. REPORTING
Weekly to CFO:
- Cash position
  A snapshot of total liquidity across all corporate bank 
  accounts and investment platforms.
- Collection performance
  Analysis of actual receipts versus the weekly target 
  and historical trends.
- Aging analysis
  A detailed breakdown of outstanding receivables categorized 
  by how long they have been overdue.

Monthly to Board:
- Cash flow statement
  A formal financial report summarizing the movement of 
  cash in and out of the company for the period.
- Variance analysis vs. forecast
  Comparison of actual cash performance against projected 
  values with explanations for significant deviations.
- Key metrics dashboard
  A visual summary of the most important cash flow performance 
  indicators for executive-level review.
